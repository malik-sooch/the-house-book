Project plan

A clean, dry side yard

Turn the bare-soil strip beside the garage-side walkway into a tidy hard surface that sheds rain away from the house, keeps weeds down, and protects the basement windows.

The goal: create a durable, low-maintenance surface without trapping water against the foundation. The finish material matters, but the slope underneath it matters even more.

What is happening now

The strip runs beside the house near the walkway to the back deck. It is bare soil, weeds return, and the basement windows make moisture control especially important. A good fix should direct surface water away from the wall, keep window wells clear, and connect neatly to the existing walk.

The non-negotiables

  • Grade away from the foundation. The surface should visibly fall away from the wall, not toward it.
  • Keep window wells functional. Do not raise the finished surface too high, block drainage, or reduce emergency-egress clearance if a window is an egress window.
  • Manage roof water first. Check gutters and downspouts so they do not discharge into this strip.
  • Protect the wall. Do not bridge siding or bury the foundation higher than it is now.
  • Plan the outlet. Water needs a safe place to go without creating ice or sending runoff onto a neighbor's property.

Four approaches to price out

Planning ranges below are rough national installed-cost estimates for a straightforward project. A narrow side yard, excavation, drainage work, frost-resistant base, access, and local labor can change the total. Get written local quotes.

1. Poured concrete

Simple + lowest upkeep

A continuous slab pitched away from the house. Easy to sweep and difficult for weeds to penetrate.

  • Pros: clean look, durable, low maintenance
  • Watch: cracking, joints, frost movement, and the detail where slab meets foundation and window wells

Estimate: about $10-$20 per sq. ft.

2. Concrete pavers

Repairable + finished look

Pavers over a compacted, frost-ready base, with edge restraint and a deliberate slope.

  • Pros: attractive; individual units can be lifted for repairs
  • Watch: higher labor cost; weeds appear if base and joint sand are poorly installed

Estimate: about $15-$30 per sq. ft.

3. Compacted gravel

Lowest initial cost

Angular gravel over geotextile with solid edging. The underlying grade must still carry water away.

  • Pros: economical, quick, easy to adjust later
  • Watch: loose stone, occasional raking, and drainage near the foundation; not a waterproof cap

Estimate: about $4-$10 per sq. ft.

4. Permeable pavers

Useful only with a drainage plan

A permeable system can reduce puddles, but infiltration right beside a basement wall may be the wrong goal.

  • Pros: polished look, less surface ponding
  • Watch: the base and underdrain must carry water away from the house; do not let it soak beside the foundation

Estimate: about $20-$35 per sq. ft.

A sensible order of work

  1. Measure the strip and photograph the wall, walkway, downspouts, window wells, and the direction water currently moves.
  2. Ask two or three contractors to assess slope and drainage before suggesting a finish.
  3. Compare written scopes, not just totals: excavation depth, base layers, final pitch, joints or edging, window-well work, runoff outlet, permits, warranty, and cleanup.
  4. Choose the finish only after the water path is clear.

Questions for every contractor

  • How will you confirm the finished surface slopes away from the foundation?
  • Where will runoff go during a heavy rain?
  • Will any window well need to be reset, extended, drained, or covered?
  • Could this work affect an egress window?
  • What excavation and compacted base do you use for Michigan freeze-thaw cycles?
  • How will the new surface meet the existing walkway and foundation?
  • Are permits or utility markings needed?
  • Can you show recent drainage or hardscape work and provide proof of insurance?
  • What is excluded from the quote, and what warranty is written into the contract?
Worth pausing for: If there are existing basement damp spots, cracks, standing water in a window well, or uncertain underground drainage, have a basement-waterproofing or drainage specialist diagnose that before covering the soil.

Best first direction

Price poured concrete and standard pavers side by side, with drainage and window-well details included in both scopes. Concrete is likely the easiest to keep clean and weed-free. Pavers may be easier to repair if future foundation or drainage access is needed. The right answer depends on the actual elevations, so the contractor should measure them before promising a solution.
